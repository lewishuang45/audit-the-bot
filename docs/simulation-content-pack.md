# Simulation Content Pack: Audit the Bot

## 1. Session Title

Audit the Bot: Launch Recommendation for CampusTea Spark

## 2. Business Brief

### Context

BrightBrew, a Hong Kong beverage startup, plans to launch a new ready-to-drink sparkling tea called CampusTea Spark. The product combines lightly carbonated jasmine tea with fruit flavoring and is positioned as a lower-sugar alternative to canned soda and bottled bubble tea.

The company wants to test the product with university students in Hong Kong before deciding whether to expand to convenience stores.

### Target Customer

The target customers are university students aged 18-24 in Hong Kong who buy drinks between classes, during study sessions, or on the way to campus activities.

### Product Options

BrightBrew is choosing one launch focus from three options:

1. Lychee Jasmine Spark
2. Peach Oolong Spark
3. Yuzu Green Tea Spark

### Budget

The launch budget is HKD 80,000 for the first month. This must cover sampling, student ambassador incentives, social media content, and basic point-of-sale materials.

### Research Findings

BrightBrew surveyed 220 university students across three Hong Kong universities.

- 64% said they would try a low-sugar sparkling tea if it were priced below HKD 18.
- 58% said taste is more important than health claims.
- 47% said they discover new drinks through friends or campus events.
- 36% said they are influenced by short-form social media videos.
- Only 18% said they regularly buy drinks online for campus delivery.
- In a blind taste test, Yuzu Green Tea scored highest on refreshment, while Lychee Jasmine scored highest on uniqueness.

### Competitor Context

- Bottled tea brands sell at HKD 10-16 in convenience stores.
- Bubble tea chains sell at HKD 24-38 and compete on customization and indulgence.
- Energy drinks sell at HKD 14-22 and compete on functional benefits.

### Distribution Constraint

For the first month, BrightBrew can distribute only through campus pop-up booths and two independent cafes near HKU and CUHK. It does not yet have convenience store access.

### Brand Constraint

BrightBrew wants a credible, student-friendly tone. The brand must not make medical or guaranteed performance claims.

### Decision Task

Recommend one launch focus and three concrete first-month actions. The recommendation must respect the budget, target students, available distribution, and research findings.

## 3. Original Flawed AI Memo

### Memo

BrightBrew should launch Lychee Jasmine Spark as a premium wellness drink for all young professionals in Hong Kong. Since 64% of surveyed students said they would buy the drink, the company can expect strong first-month sales if it moves quickly into convenience stores and food delivery platforms.

The best strategy is to position CampusTea Spark as a productivity booster that helps students stay focused during exams. The product should be priced at HKD 24 to signal quality and compete directly with bubble tea. BrightBrew should spend most of the HKD 80,000 budget on paid influencer campaigns with citywide lifestyle creators because social media is clearly the strongest discovery channel.

For launch actions, BrightBrew should first partner with major convenience store chains to secure shelf placement across Hong Kong. Second, it should run a two-week "Drink Better, Study Better" campaign promising that the tea helps students concentrate. Third, it should offer free delivery bundles through online platforms so students can order cases to campus.

This plan is low risk because Hong Kong students are already moving away from sugary drinks, and Lychee Jasmine's unique flavor will guarantee word-of-mouth growth. The company should not focus on campus booths because booths are slow and old-fashioned compared with digital marketing.

The recommendation should target Hong Kong university students aged 18-24, respect the HKD 80,000 first-month budget, and use only campus pop-up booths plus the two cafes near HKU and CUHK as distribution channels.

Recommend one launch flavor and three concrete first-month actions. Keep the price below HKD 18 because the survey says 64% would try the drink only under that threshold. Explain tradeoffs between Yuzu Green Tea and Lychee Jasmine using the taste-test results. ## 7. Sample Revised AI Memo

BrightBrew should launch Yuzu Green Tea Spark as the first-month focus for Hong Kong university students. The strongest reason is that Yuzu Green Tea scored highest on refreshment in the blind taste test, and the product is meant to be consumed between classes, during study breaks, or around campus activities. Lychee Jasmine is distinctive, but uniqueness alone is less directly connected to repeat trial than refreshment for an everyday campus drink.

The product should be priced at HKD 16-18 to fit the survey finding that 64% of students would try a low-sugar sparkling tea below HKD 18. The launch should avoid medical or performance claims. Instead of promising better concentration, BrightBrew should use a credible message such as "light, sparkling tea for study breaks."

First, BrightBrew should run sampling booths at selected campus locations during lunch and late-afternoon periods, when students are likely to buy drinks between classes. Second, it should recruit student ambassadors to bring small tasting groups to the booths and cafes, because 47% of surveyed students discover new drinks through friends or campus events. Third, it should create short-form videos around taste reactions and campus moments, but keep social spending focused because only 36% cited short-form videos as a discovery source.

This plan fits the first-month distribution limits because it uses campus pop-up booths and the two independent cafes near HKU and CUHK rather than assuming convenience store access. It also keeps the campaign within the brand's student-friendly tone and avoids unsupported claims about guaranteed sales or academic performance.

## 8. Sample Final Memo

### Final Recommendation

BrightBrew should launch Yuzu Green Tea Spark first, priced at HKD 16-18, and position it as a refreshing low-sugar sparkling tea for campus breaks. This choice is better supported than Lychee Jasmine because Yuzu Green Tea scored highest on refreshment, which fits the target use case of students buying drinks between classes or during study sessions. Lychee Jasmine's uniqueness is useful for future rotation, but the first launch should prioritize broad trial and repeatability.

### Three First-Month Actions

1. Run campus pop-up sampling during lunch and late-afternoon periods at locations near high student traffic.
2. Use student ambassadors to invite friend groups to tasting sessions and collect quick flavor feedback.
3. Create short-form campus videos showing taste reactions and booth moments, with limited paid boosting rather than a citywide influencer campaign.

### Rationale

This plan respects the HKD 80,000 budget by focusing on sampling, ambassadors, and targeted content instead of broad influencer spending. It also follows the available distribution channels: campus booths and two independent cafes near HKU and CUHK. The message should avoid claims about concentration or productivity and instead use a credible line such as "light sparkling tea for study breaks."

### AI Content Decisions

Accepted: The AI correctly noticed that Lychee Jasmine has a uniqueness advantage, but this is not enough to make it the first launch flavor.

Revised: The AI's social media recommendation was narrowed from citywide influencer spending to targeted short-form content because campus events and friends are a stronger discovery source in the survey.

Rejected: The AI's suggestions about convenience stores, HKD 24 pricing, productivity claims, guaranteed word-of-mouth, and online delivery bundles were rejected because they conflict with the brief or lack evidence.

## 9. Rubric Scoring Anchors

### Verify Accuracy: 0-3

- 0: Does not identify meaningful problems in the AI memo, or mostly accepts flawed claims.
- 1: Identifies one or two obvious problems but misses major factual or constraint errors.
- 2: Identifies several important problems, including at least one factual error and one constraint mismatch.
- 3: Accurately identifies most major problems, including factual errors, unsupported claims, distribution mismatch, pricing issue, and ethical/tone risk.

### Modification Quality: 0-3

- 0: Final memo is unclear, unsupported, or still repeats major AI errors.
- 1: Final memo improves wording but still has weak evidence or important misalignment with the brief.
- 2: Final memo is mostly clear and relevant, with concrete actions and only minor gaps.
- 3: Final memo is clear, evidence-based, feasible within constraints, and directly tied to the brief.

### Guidance Effectiveness: 0-2

- 0: Prompt is vague, generic, or asks AI to "make it better" without diagnosis.
- 1: Prompt mentions some specific problems or constraints but is incomplete.
- 2: Prompt clearly names flaws, constraints, desired structure, evidence boundaries, and tone requirements.

### Quality of Argumentation: 0-2

- 0: Does not explain why AI content was accepted, revised, or rejected.
- 1: Gives some explanation, but reasoning is thin or disconnected from the brief.
- 2: Clearly explains accept/revise/reject decisions using case facts and constraints.
